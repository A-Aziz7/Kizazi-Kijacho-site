Privacy, legal notice and accessibility

Three statements Swiss law requires of a site like this one. The text below is a draft structure; it must be drafted and cleared by UZH Legal Services and the university's data protection officer before publishing.

Privacy policy

Required by Art. 19 of the revised Federal Act on Data Protection, in force since 1 September 2023. It must be reachable in one click from every page, which is why it sits in the footer of all pages.

Controller and contact

Who is responsible for the data, and the address for data protection enquiries.

What is collected, and why

Server logs, any analytics, and data submitted through the contact address, each with its purpose.

Recipients and transfers abroad

Google Sites hosting and YouTube embeds mean data reaches the United States, which the FDPIC does not treat as adequate on its own. The safeguard relied on has to be named.

Cookies and analytics

What Google sets, what it is for, and how to object. Swiss law works on opt-out; visitors from the EU need opt-in for anything beyond essential cookies.

Your rights

Access, correction, deletion and objection, and how to exercise them.

Retention and version

How long data is kept, plus a dated version line at the foot of the policy.

Research participant data from the Dodoma trial is governed by the study's own ethics approvals and consent forms, not by this policy. This is stated explicitly here so the two are not confused.

Legal notice

Art. 3 of the Unfair Competition Act requires a clear statement of who operates the site and how to reach them. A named publisher, a postal address and an email address are the minimum. A copyright line for the site's text and photographs belongs here too, together with the credit terms for any photographs supplied by partners.

Accessibility statement

The Disability Discrimination Act (BehiG) binds public bodies, and the Swiss standard eCH-0059 v3 sets the technical bar at WCAG 2.1 level AA. Federal and cantonal sites publish a dated statement naming the standard, listing known exceptions, and giving a barrier-reporting address. A revision extending the duty to private service providers is expected in 2027.

Standard applied

eCH-0059 v3, WCAG 2.1 level AA. State whether the site is fully or partially conformant.

Known exceptions

Embedded YouTube players and any PDF reports that have not been remediated. Both are the usual exceptions on university sites.

Report a barrier

An email address that reaches someone who can act, plus the date the statement was issued and last reviewed.
