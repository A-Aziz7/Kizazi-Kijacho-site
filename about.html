About the program

A long-term research program, built to keep going

How the program came about

Poor developmental outcomes early in life can have a lasting impact and pass poverty and marginalisation from one generation to the next. A great deal is known about what helps children develop — the provision of nurturing care; far less is known about the constraints in the delivery of nurturing care, how to sustainability relax these constraints and change caregiving practices, what works at scale, at what cost, and in what sequence — the questions governments actually face.

Kizazi Kijacho was launched in March 2021 to close that gap in Tanzania. Led by Bet Caeyers of Chr. Michelsen Institute (Norway) and Ingvild Almås of the University of Zurich (Switzerland) and Stockholm University (Sweden), it brings together an international, multi-displiniary team of researchers (e.g., economists, public health scientists, nutritionists, child psychologists, epidemiologists) and implementing agencies, working in close collaboration with the Tanzanian government.

Where we are heading

We have completed a randomised controlled trial in Dodoma region and its data are being analysed. The households will continue to be followed, and further lab experiments and a follow-up at school entry are being prepared. A nationally representative longitudinal study is also planned.

Field experiments
Cluster-randomised trials testing interventions at scale.
02
Lab-in-the-field experiments
A lab-in-the-field experiment on father engagement and household spending on children.
03
Longitudinal surveys
A planned, nationally representative cohort of children followed over time.
04
Implementation science
Mixed-methods research focused on the feasibility and sustainability of early childhood interventions (e.g. process evaluations, costing studies).

Timeline

2021

Partnerships established. Program launched at IIES, Stockholm University. Study design and instrument development.

2022

Cluster-randomised trial launched in Dodoma. Baseline survey during pregnancy across 387 villages.

2022 — 2025

Parenting and cash transfer intervention delivery, lab-in-the-field experiments, Age 1, and Age 2 data collection for the Dodoma trial.

2025 —

Analysis, publication and dissemination. Launch of the nationally representative longitudinal study. Program now hosted at the University of Zurich.
