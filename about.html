About the program

A long-term research program, built to keep going

Photo — team or fieldwork, wide crop

How the program came about

Poor developmental outcomes in the first five years can last a lifetime and pass poverty and marginalisation from one generation to the next. A great deal is known about what helps children develop; far less is known about what works at scale, at what cost, and in what sequence — the questions governments actually face.

Kizazi Kijacho was launched in March 2021 to close that gap in Tanzania. It brought together economists, epidemiologists and implementers from seven research institutes across four continents, working alongside a Tanzanian implementation team and the National Multisectoral Early Childhood Development Programme.

Where we are heading

The trial in Dodoma is complete and its data are being analysed. The cohort continues to be followed, and further lab experiments and a follow-up at school entry are being prepared. The program is now led from the University of Zurich, in continued collaboration with the Institute for International Economic Studies in Stockholm.

The aim over the coming years is to turn the evidence into something usable: cost estimates per child, guidance on delivery through existing health and community structures, and measurement tools that governments can run themselves.

Timeline

2021

Program launched at IIES, Stockholm University. Study design and instrument development.

2022

Baseline survey across 390 villages. Cluster-randomised trial launched in Dodoma.

2023 — 2024

Intervention delivery, lab-in-the-field experiments, endline data collection.

2025 —

Analysis, publication and dissemination. Program hosted at the University of Zurich.

Funded by the Swedish Research Council, the European Research Council and the Swiss National Science Foundation, with earlier support from the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office as part of the Thrive research programme.
